<div class="note-content" markdown="0">

<p>Global complexity bounds measure the entire class, including functions far from the part relevant to estimation. Localization restricts attention to a ball in the population or empirical quadratic norm. The radius must be chosen consistently with the fluctuation bound inside that ball. A critical-radius inequality expresses this balance and lets us compare empirical norms with population norms.</p>

<p>Let {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(X_1,\ldots,X_n\)</span>{% endraw %} be i.i.d. with law {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(P\)</span>{% endraw %}. For a function {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(f\)</span>{% endraw %}, write {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(Pf=\mathbb{E} f(X_1)\)</span>{% endraw %}, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(P_nf=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n f(X_i)\)</span>{% endraw %}, and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathbb G_nf=\sqrt n(P_n-P)f\)</span>{% endraw %}. Rademacher variables are independent random signs, independent of the observations. See the <a href="{% post_url 2022-09-03-empirical-processes-tensorization-and-the-entropy-method %}">empirical-process setup</a> for measurability conventions.</p>

<div id="localization-and-the-uniform-law" data-note-section="title">

</div>
<p>We compare the empirical norm {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\|f\|_n=(P_nf^2)^{1/2}\)</span>{% endraw %} with {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\|f\|_P=(Pf^2)^{1/2}\)</span>{% endraw %}. Assume the class is star-shaped about zero and uniformly bounded by {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(b&gt;0\)</span>{% endraw %}. All the localized suprema below are assumed measurable and determined by countable subclasses, as for the usual pointwise measurable separable classes. The case {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(b=0\)</span>{% endraw %} is immediate. We first use {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(b=1\)</span>{% endraw %}; rescaling gives the general case.<a href="#fn1" class="footnote-ref" id="fnref1" role="doc-noteref"><sup>1</sup></a></p>
<p>Three quantities must be distinguished:</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\begin{aligned}R(t)&amp;=\mathbb E_X\mathbb E_\varepsilon\sup_{\|f\|_P\le t}\left|\frac1n\sum_i\varepsilon_if(X_i)\right|,\\A_X(t)&amp;=\mathbb E_\varepsilon\sup_{\|f\|_P\le t}\left|\frac1n\sum_i\varepsilon_if(X_i)\right|,\\\widehat R_X(t)&amp;=\mathbb E_\varepsilon\sup_{\|f\|_n\le t}\left|\frac1n\sum_i\varepsilon_if(X_i)\right|.\end{aligned}\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>Every supremum is over {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(f\in\mathcal F\)</span>{% endraw %}. Thus {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(R(t)=\mathbb E_XA_X(t)\)</span>{% endraw %}, while {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\widehat R_X\)</span>{% endraw %} localizes using the empirical norm. In particular, conditioning on the observations does not by itself change a population ball into an empirical ball. The normalization {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(1/n\)</span>{% endraw %} is used throughout.</p>
<p>Fix the numerical constant {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(A=128\)</span>{% endraw %}. Define critical radii by</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\delta_n=\inf\{t&gt;0:R(t)\le t^2/(Ab)\},\qquad \widehat\delta_n=\inf\{t&gt;0:\widehat R_X(t)\le t^2/(Ab)\}.\tag{1}\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>The symbols {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(R,\widehat R_X\)</span>{% endraw %} replace {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\overline{\mathcal R}_n,\breve{\mathcal R}_n\)</span>{% endraw %}, respectively. Star-shapedness implies that each complexity is nondecreasing and its ratio to {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t\)</span>{% endraw %} is nonincreasing. Indeed, if {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(0&lt;u\le t\)</span>{% endraw %}, then {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\((u/t)f\in\mathcal F\)</span>{% endraw %} and its relevant norm is at most {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(u\)</span>{% endraw %} whenever the norm of {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(f\)</span>{% endraw %} is at most {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t\)</span>{% endraw %}. Taking suprema and expectations gives</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\frac utR(t)\le R(u),\qquad \frac ut\widehat R_X(t)\le\widehat R_X(u).\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>Together with monotonicity this proves continuity at every positive radius. The admissible radii form an upper interval, since {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(R(t)/t^2\)</span>{% endraw %} is nonincreasing. As the complexities are bounded by {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(b\)</span>{% endraw %}, this interval is nonempty. At a positive critical radius equality holds in (1); at every larger radius the defining inequality holds. The convention {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\inf=0\)</span>{% endraw %} handles a degenerate zero class.</p>
<p><strong>Concentration theorem.</strong> We use the two-sided bounded empirical-process concentration theorem of <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0506594">Klein and Rio (2005), Theorems 1.1–1.2, pp. 2–3 of the author reprint</a>. For a countable class of mean-zero functions bounded in absolute value by one, let {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(Z\)</span>{% endraw %} be the supremum of their sums and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(v=\sup\sum_i\operatorname{Var}(h(X_i))+2\mathbb EZ\)</span>{% endraw %}. Their Laplace-transform bounds imply, for universal constants,</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\log\mathbb E e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb EZ)}\le C v\lambda^2\quad(|\lambda|\le c),\qquad P(|Z-\mathbb EZ|&gt;x)\le2\exp\{-cx^2/(v+x)\}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>The second form follows by Chernoff with {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(|\lambda|\)</span>{% endraw %} the smaller of a fixed constant and a constant times {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(x/v\)</span>{% endraw %}. Absolute suprema are covered by adjoining negatives. We use this concentration theorem without proof; the normalization, variance estimates, and localization arguments below are proved here.</p>
<span id="eq:critical-inequality-rademacher"></span><span id="eq:critical-inequality-rademacher-emp"></span>
<div id="lem:sandwich-rademacher-radii" class="lemma" data-note-number="1" data-note-kind="Lemma">
<p><strong>Lemma 1 (comparison of critical radii).</strong> For the bounded star-shaped class and the normalization (1), there are universal {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(c_1,c_2&gt;0\)</span>{% endraw %} such that</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\delta_n/4\le\widehat\delta_n\le3\delta_n\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>with probability at least {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(1-c_1e^{-c_2n\delta_n^2/b^2}\)</span>{% endraw %}.</p>
</div>
<div class="proof">
<p><em>Proof.</em> Work first with {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(b=1\)</span>{% endraw %} and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\delta=\delta_n&gt;0\)</span>{% endraw %}. Theorem 2 below, whose proof is independent of this lemma, gives with probability at least {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(1-Ce^{-cn\delta^2}\)</span>{% endraw %} the event</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[B_1=\left\{\sup_f\frac{|\|f\|_n^2-\|f\|_P^2|}{\|f\|_P^2+\delta^2}\le\frac12\right\}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>On this event, rearranging the squared inequalities gives {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\|f\|_P^2\le2\|f\|_n^2+\delta^2\)</span>{% endraw %} and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\|f\|_n^2\le(3/2)\|f\|_P^2+\delta^2/2\)</span>{% endraw %}. In particular each norm is at most twice the other plus {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\delta\)</span>{% endraw %}. Hence</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[A_X(t)\le\widehat R_X(2t+\delta),\qquad \widehat R_X(t)\le A_X(2t+\delta).\tag{2}\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>For the other needed event, apply the concentration theorem on the product space of observations and signs to {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\{(x,e)\mapsto\pm ef(x):\|f\|_P\le t\}\)</span>{% endraw %}. The summands are centered, bounded by one, and have variance at most {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t^2\)</span>{% endraw %}. If {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(Z_t\)</span>{% endraw %} is their supremum of sums, then {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathbb EZ_t=nR(t)\)</span>{% endraw %} and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathbb E_\varepsilon Z_t=nA_X(t)\)</span>{% endraw %}. For either sign of {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\lambda\)</span>{% endraw %}, conditional Jensen gives</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\mathbb E_Xe^{\lambda n(A_X(t)-R(t))}\le\mathbb E_{X,\varepsilon}e^{\lambda(Z_t-nR(t))}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>Thus the same Laplace bound, followed by Chernoff, yields</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[P(|A_X(t)-R(t)|&gt;u)\le2\exp\left\{-\frac{cnu^2}{t^2+2R(t)+u}\right\}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>For {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t=\alpha\delta\)</span>{% endraw %}, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\alpha\ge1\)</span>{% endraw %}, star-shapedness gives the needed <em>upper</em> bound {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(R(t)\le\alpha\delta^2/A\)</span>{% endraw %}. Taking {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(u=\alpha\delta^2/(8A)\)</span>{% endraw %} shows that</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[B_0(\alpha\delta)=\{|A_X(\alpha\delta)-R(\alpha\delta)|\le\alpha\delta^2/(8A)\}\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>fails with probability at most {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(2e^{-c'n\delta^2}\)</span>{% endraw %}, where the fixed {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(A\)</span>{% endraw %} is absorbed into the universal constant. We need only {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\alpha=1,7\)</span>{% endraw %}.</p>
<p>On {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(B_1\cap B_0(7\delta)\)</span>{% endraw %},</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\widehat R_X(3\delta)\le A_X(7\delta)\le\frac{7\delta^2}{A}+\frac{7\delta^2}{8A}&lt;\frac{(3\delta)^2}{A}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>So {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\widehat\delta_n\le3\delta\)</span>{% endraw %}. On {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(B_0(\delta)\)</span>{% endraw %}, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(A_X(\delta)\ge7\delta^2/(8A)&gt;0\)</span>{% endraw %}; (2) then implies {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\widehat\delta_n&gt;0\)</span>{% endraw %}. Since {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(3\delta\ge\widehat\delta_n\)</span>{% endraw %}, the scaling inequality and the equality at the empirical critical radius give<a href="#fn2" class="footnote-ref" id="fnref2" role="doc-noteref"><sup>2</sup></a></p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\frac{\delta^2}{A}=R(\delta)\le A_X(\delta)+\frac{\delta^2}{8A}\le\widehat R_X(3\delta)+\frac{\delta^2}{8A}\le\frac{3\delta\widehat\delta_n}{A}+\frac{\delta^2}{8A}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>Therefore {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\widehat\delta_n\ge7\delta/24&gt;\delta/4\)</span>{% endraw %}. A union bound proves the asserted probability. If {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\delta_n=0\)</span>{% endraw %}, scaling from arbitrarily small admissible radii gives {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(R(t)=0\)</span>{% endraw %} for every {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t&gt;0\)</span>{% endraw %}; countable determination then implies all sample evaluations vanish almost surely, so {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\widehat\delta_n=0\)</span>{% endraw %} as well. Finally apply the result to {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathcal F/b\)</span>{% endraw %}, whose critical radii are {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\delta_n/b\)</span>{% endraw %} and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\widehat\delta_n/b\)</span>{% endraw %}. ◻</p>
</div>
<span id="eq:mathcal-A-conditional-bound"></span><span id="eq:breve-R-n-conditional-bound"></span>
<div id="thm:bounding-rademacher-complexity-star-shaped-c-unif-bouned" class="theorem" data-note-number="2" data-note-kind="Theorem">
<p><strong>Theorem 2 (localized comparison of quadratic norms).</strong> Let {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(|f|\le b\)</span>{% endraw %}, let {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathcal F\)</span>{% endraw %} be star-shaped, and let {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\delta&gt;0\)</span>{% endraw %} satisfy {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(R(\delta)\le\delta^2/(128b)\)</span>{% endraw %}. For every {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t\ge\delta\)</span>{% endraw %}, with probability at least {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(1-Ce^{-cnt^2/b^2}\)</span>{% endraw %}, simultaneously for all {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(f\in\mathcal F\)</span>{% endraw %},</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[|\|f\|_n^2-\|f\|_P^2|\le\frac12\max\{\|f\|_P^2,t^2\}\le\frac{\|f\|_P^2+t^2}{2}.\tag{3}\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>This is a statement about <em>squared</em> norms. For the norms themselves, if {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(0&lt;\delta&lt;b\)</span>{% endraw %} and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(m=\lceil\log_2(b/\delta)\rceil\)</span>{% endraw %}, then</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[P\left(\sup_f|\|f\|_n-\|f\|_P|&gt;\delta\right)\le C(1+m)e^{-cn\delta^2/b^2}.\tag{4}\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>In particular, if {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(cn\delta^2/(2b^2)\ge\log(1+m)\)</span>{% endraw %}, the right side is at most {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(Ce^{-cn\delta^2/(2b^2)}\)</span>{% endraw %}. If {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\delta\ge b\)</span>{% endraw %}, the norm comparison is deterministic.</p>
</div>
<div class="proof">
<p><em>Proof.</em> Normalize to {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(b=1\)</span>{% endraw %}, and define the squared-norm fluctuation</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[M_n(r)=\sup_{f\in\mathcal F:\|f\|_P\le r}|(P_n-P)f^2|.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>For {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(r\ge\delta\)</span>{% endraw %}, symmetrization and the Rademacher contraction inequality on {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\([-1,1]\)</span>{% endraw %} give</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\mathbb EM_n(r)\le2\mathbb E\sup_{\|f\|_P\le r}\left|n^{-1}\sum_i\varepsilon_if(X_i)^2\right|\le8R(r)\le\frac{r\delta}{16}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>We use the conservative contraction constant four for the absolute supremum: {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(x\mapsto x^2\)</span>{% endraw %} is two-Lipschitz on {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\([-1,1]\)</span>{% endraw %}, and passing from a one-sided supremum to an absolute one costs at most another factor two. The preceding uniform-entropy note proves this contraction step. The last inequality is exactly star-shaped scaling and the critical inequality; the factor {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(r\)</span>{% endraw %} must be retained.</p>
<p>For the centered functions {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\pm(f^2-Pf^2)\)</span>{% endraw %}, the absolute bound is one and the variance is at most {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(Pf^4\le Pf^2\le r^2\)</span>{% endraw %}. Applying the concentration theorem to {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(nM_n(r)\)</span>{% endraw %} gives</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[P(M_n(r)&gt;\mathbb EM_n(r)+u)\le2\exp\left\{-\frac{cnu^2}{r^2+2\mathbb EM_n(r)+u}\right\}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>Take {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(u=s^2/8\)</span>{% endraw %}. Since {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(r\ge\delta\)</span>{% endraw %}, the expectation is at most {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(r^2/16\)</span>{% endraw %}, and its sum with {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(s^2/8\)</span>{% endraw %} is at most {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\((r\delta+s^2)/4\)</span>{% endraw %}. Consequently, for {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(r,s\ge\delta\)</span>{% endraw %},</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[P\left(M_n(r)&gt;\frac{r\delta+s^2}{4}\right)\le2e^{-c'n\min\{s^2,s^4/r^2\}}.\tag{5}\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p><strong>From one ball to the relative bound.</strong> Take {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(r=s=t\)</span>{% endraw %} in (5). Except on an event of probability at most {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(2e^{-c'nt^2}\)</span>{% endraw %}, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(M_n(t)\le t^2/2\)</span>{% endraw %}. If {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(a=\|f\|_P\le t\)</span>{% endraw %}, this gives {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(|(P_n-P)f^2|\le t^2/2\)</span>{% endraw %}. If {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(a&gt;t\)</span>{% endraw %}, star-shapedness puts {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(g=(t/a)f\)</span>{% endraw %} in the radius-{% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t\)</span>{% endraw %} ball, so</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[|(P_n-P)f^2|=(a/t)^2|(P_n-P)g^2|\le a^2/2.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>This proves (3) uniformly over the entire class using just one ball. The multiplier {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t/a\)</span>{% endraw %} lies in {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\([0,1]\)</span>{% endraw %}; scaling a function upwards is not justified by star-shapedness.</p>
<p><strong>From squared norms to absolute norm error.</strong> First apply (5) with {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(r=s=\delta\)</span>{% endraw %}, so {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(M_n(\delta)\le\delta^2/2\)</span>{% endraw %} outside a set of probability {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(2e^{-c'n\delta^2}\)</span>{% endraw %}. For each {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(j=1,\ldots,m\)</span>{% endraw %}, set {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(r_j=2^j\delta\)</span>{% endraw %} and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(s_j^2=r_j\delta\)</span>{% endraw %}. Formula (5) gives {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(M_n(r_j)\le r_j\delta/2\)</span>{% endraw %}, with failure probability at most {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(2e^{-c'n\delta^2}\)</span>{% endraw %} for each {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(j\)</span>{% endraw %}. Indeed, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(s_j^4/r_j^2=\delta^2\)</span>{% endraw %} and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(s_j^2\ge\delta^2\)</span>{% endraw %}.</p>
<p>On the intersection of these events, if {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(a\le\delta\)</span>{% endraw %}, then</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[|\|f\|_n-a|^2\le|\|f\|_n^2-a^2|\le\delta^2/2.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>If {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(r_j/2&lt;a\le r_j\)</span>{% endraw %}, then</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[|\|f\|_n-a|=\frac{|\|f\|_n^2-a^2|}{\|f\|_n+a}\le\frac{r_j\delta}{2a}\le\delta.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>These deterministic shells cover every population radius up to one. A union bound over the {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(m+1\)</span>{% endraw %} events proves (4). The displayed condition on {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(n\delta^2\)</span>{% endraw %} absorbs the number of shells. For {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(b\ne1\)</span>{% endraw %}, apply the proof to {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(f/b\)</span>{% endraw %}, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(r/b\)</span>{% endraw %}, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\delta/b\)</span>{% endraw %}, and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t/b\)</span>{% endraw %}; both sides of (3) scale by {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(b^2\)</span>{% endraw %}, while the norm error scales by {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(b\)</span>{% endraw %}. ◻</p>
</div>

<div id="rem:direct-argument" class="remark" data-note-number="3" data-note-kind="Remark">
<p><strong>Remark 3 (why the critical-radius bounds scale).</strong> The inequalities used in Lemma 1 are deterministic consequences of star-shapedness, not additional tail estimates.<a href="#fn3" class="footnote-ref" id="fnref3" role="doc-noteref"><sup>3</sup></a> For example, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\widehat R_X(t)/t\le\widehat R_X(u)/u\)</span>{% endraw %} for {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t\ge u&gt;0\)</span>{% endraw %}. At a positive empirical critical radius this gives {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\widehat R_X(t)\le t\widehat\delta_n/(Ab)\)</span>{% endraw %} whenever {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t\ge\widehat\delta_n\)</span>{% endraw %}; the population version is identical. Concentration is needed only to compare {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(A_X\)</span>{% endraw %} with its mean and to compare the two norms. The proof above establishes those events before using them.</p>
</div>
<div id="ex:bounding-rademacher-complexity-quadratic" class="example" data-note-number="4" data-note-kind="Example">
<p><strong>Example 4 (quadratic polynomials).</strong> Let {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(P\)</span>{% endraw %} be the uniform probability law on {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\([-1,1]\)</span>{% endraw %} and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathcal P_2=\{a_0+a_1x+a_2x^2:\|f\|_\infty\le1\}\)</span>{% endraw %}. An orthonormal basis for {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(L^2(P)\)</span>{% endraw %} on this subspace is</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\phi_0(x)=1,\qquad\phi_1(x)=\sqrt3x,\qquad\phi_2(x)=\frac{\sqrt5}{2}(3x^2-1).\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>These are normalized for the probability measure {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(dx/2\)</span>{% endraw %}. If {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(f_\eta=\eta^\top\phi\)</span>{% endraw %}, then {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\|f_\eta\|_P=\|\eta\|_2\)</span>{% endraw %}. Let {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(D_{ij}=\phi_j(X_i)\)</span>{% endraw %}, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(j=0,1,2\)</span>{% endraw %}. Cauchy–Schwarz and Jensen give</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[R(t)\le\frac tn\mathbb E\|D^\top\varepsilon\|_2\le\frac tn\sqrt{\mathbb E\|D^\top\varepsilon\|_2^2}=t\sqrt{3/n}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>The final equality follows from {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathbb E_\varepsilon\|D^\top\varepsilon\|_2^2=\operatorname{tr}(D^\top D)\)</span>{% endraw %} and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}(D^\top D)=3n\)</span>{% endraw %}. Thus {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\delta=128\sqrt{3/n}\)</span>{% endraw %} satisfies the critical inequality. This is obtained by solving {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t\sqrt{3/n}\le t^2/128\)</span>{% endraw %}; the radius on the right is not a second independent parameter.</p>
<p>For this fixed three-dimensional class, a direct Gram-matrix argument proves the sharper uniform {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(O_P(n^{-1/2})\)</span>{% endraw %} conclusion without the general peeling condition. Set {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(G_n=D^\top D/n\)</span>{% endraw %}, so {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathbb EG_n=I_3\)</span>{% endraw %}. Since {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\|\eta\|_2\le1\)</span>{% endraw %} for {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(f_\eta\in\mathcal P_2\)</span>{% endraw %},</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\sup_{f_\eta\in\mathcal P_2}\left|\sqrt{\eta^\top G_n\eta}-\|\eta\|_2\right|\le\|G_n-I_3\|_{\mathrm{op}}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>For {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\eta\ne0\)</span>{% endraw %}, divide the difference of squares by {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\sqrt{\eta^\top G_n\eta}+\|\eta\|_2\ge\|\eta\|_2\)</span>{% endraw %}; {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\eta=0\)</span>{% endraw %} is immediate. Each of the nine entries of {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(G_n-I_3\)</span>{% endraw %} is an average of bounded, centered variables. Hoeffding and {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le3\max_{j,k}|H_{jk}|\)</span>{% endraw %} give a bound {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(18e^{-cnt^2}\)</span>{% endraw %} for exceeding {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t\)</span>{% endraw %}, with a universal {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(c&gt;0\)</span>{% endraw %}. Thus the uniform norm error is {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(O_P(n^{-1/2})\)</span>{% endraw %}.</p>
<p>This order cannot be improved to {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(o_P(n^{-1/2})\)</span>{% endraw %}: the single function {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(f(x)=x\)</span>{% endraw %} belongs to the class, and the scalar CLT and delta method give</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\sqrt n\left(\sqrt{P_nX^2}-\sqrt{1/3}\right)\Rightarrow \mathcal{N}(0,1/15),\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>because {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\operatorname{Var}(X^2)=1/5-1/9=4/45\)</span>{% endraw %}.</p>
</div>
<div id="result-5" class="remark" data-note-number="5" data-note-kind="Remark">
<p><strong>Remark 5.</strong> Localization keeps the radius factor in {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(R(t)\le t\sqrt{3/n}\)</span>{% endraw %}. A global complexity bound also proves a uniform law, but by itself does not give the same control near zero in the population norm. In the quadratic example, the Gram matrix gives a direct route to the uniform root-{% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(n\)</span>{% endraw %} rate.</p>
</div>
<div id="result-6" class="proposition" data-note-number="6" data-note-kind="Proposition">
<p><strong>Proposition 6 (expected empirical radius).</strong> Let {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathcal F\)</span>{% endraw %} be uniformly bounded by one and star-shaped, and let {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(R(\delta)\le\delta^2/128\)</span>{% endraw %}. Then, for all {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(t\ge\delta\)</span>{% endraw %},</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\mathbb E\sup_{\|f\|_P\le t}\|f\|_n\le\sqrt{17/16}\,t\le\sqrt5\,t.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>Uniform boundedness, rather than Lipschitz continuity on the sample space, is the hypothesis needed here.</p>
</div>
<div class="proof">
<p><em>Proof.</em> Pointwise, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\sup_{\|f\|_P\le t}P_nf^2\le t^2+M_n(t)\)</span>{% endraw %}. Jensen and the mean bound already proved give</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\mathbb E\sup_{\|f\|_P\le t}\|f\|_n\le\sqrt{\mathbb E\sup_{\|f\|_P\le t}P_nf^2}\le\sqrt{t^2+8R(t)}\le\sqrt{t^2+t\delta/16}\le\sqrt{17/16}\,t.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>No square of an expected supremum of squared-norm errors is needed. ◻</p>
</div>
<div id="rem:example-bounding-rademacher-complexity-quadratic" class="remark" data-note-number="7" data-note-kind="Remark">
<p><strong>Remark 7 (conditional global complexity).</strong> For a fixed sample, {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(Z_f=n^{-1}\sum_i\varepsilon_if(X_i)\)</span>{% endraw %} has increments satisfying</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\mathbb E_\varepsilon e^{\lambda(Z_f-Z_g)}=\prod_i\cosh\!\left(\frac\lambda n(f(X_i)-g(X_i))\right)\le\exp\left\{\frac{\lambda^2}{2n}\|f-g\|_n^2\right\}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>This mgf calculation proves the subgaussian property; a variance calculation alone would not. Its increment variance is exactly {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\|f-g\|_n^2/n\)</span>{% endraw %}.</p>
<p>The evaluation vectors of {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\mathcal P_2\)</span>{% endraw %} lie in the column space of {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(D\)</span>{% endraw %}, of dimension {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(d_n\le3\)</span>{% endraw %}, and in its empirical unit ball. Work in this image space, which removes any kernel of {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(D\)</span>{% endraw %}; the coefficient-to-evaluation map need not be an isomorphism. The Euclidean packing-volume argument yields</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\log N(u,\mathcal P_2,\|\cdot\|_n)\le d_n\log(1+2/u)\le3\log(1+2/u).\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>Dudley's inequality for the signed class, which contains zero, now gives</p>
{% raw %}<div class="math display">\[\mathbb E_\varepsilon\sup_{f\in\mathcal P_2}|Z_f|\le\frac C{\sqrt n}\int_0^2\sqrt{\log2+3\log(1+2/u)}du\le\frac{C'}{\sqrt n}.\]</div>{% endraw %}
<p>The integral is finite: near zero its integrand is bounded by a constant times {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(\sqrt{1+\log(1/u)}\)</span>{% endraw %}, whose integral converges under {% raw %}<span class="math inline">\(u=e^{-v}\)</span>{% endraw %}. This holds for every sample. It is a global bound and complements, rather than improves, the radius-dependent population bound in Example 4.</p>
</div>
